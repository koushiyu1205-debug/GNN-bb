# Style Profile

This profile governs later English drafting. It does not authorize drafting
before the controlling motivation is confirmed.

| Style Dimension | Target Venue Expectation | Exemplar Pattern | Applied To This Paper |
|---|---|---|---|
| Transportation framing | The emerging technology must be connected to transportation-system planning, operation, safety, reliability, or resource use. | The TRC exemplars begin from an operational system and introduce the algorithm as the means to resolve a system-level decision problem. | Present lunar water-ice prospecting as resource-constrained autonomous fleet transportation over a remote exploration network. Local navigation, sensing, and GAT technology remain supporting components. |
| Controlling contribution | A journal paper should sustain one precise contribution rather than a collection of unrelated engineering changes. | Exact-routing exemplars connect a distinctive route structure to one tailored solution architecture. | Center the paper on pricing-led learning guidance with secondary branch-candidate ranking inside exact BPC. Keep cut management deterministic and outside the learning contribution. |
| Title and abstract | Both the transportation problem and the methodological contribution should be visible. | Exemplar titles join the routing variant with “route-based,” “branch-and-price,” “branch-price-and-cut,” or another clear solution class. | Name lunar water-ice exploration fleet routing and learning-guided exact branch-price-and-cut. Do not put an unverified speedup, solve scale, or “first” claim in the title or abstract. |
| Introduction logic | Narrow from an operational consequence to a modeling or algorithmic gap. | The exemplars progress from system need, to coupled constraints, to limits of existing methods, and then to the exact contribution. | Move from prospecting-site coverage and resource/risk constraints to multi-sortie fleet decisions requiring exact proofs, then to the computational burden of exact BPC and the need for proof-preserving learning guidance. |
| Method exposition | Model and algorithm components should be introduced in dependency order. | Route-based exemplars define columns and the master problem before pricing, inequalities, branching, and acceleration. | Present the fixed path-option space and journey model first, followed by the RMP, native exact SPPRC, deterministic live-SRI machinery, Ryan–Foster branching, learned pricing ranker, secondary learned branch ranker, exact fallbacks, and proof audit. |
| Exactness language | Formal guarantees must be traceable to explicit mathematical or algorithmic mechanisms. | The closest BPC exemplar keeps pricing, cuts, branching, and optimality proofs visible. | Reserve “proves,” “proves,” “exact,” and “optimal” for the exact path and the fixed logical-graph solution space. Describe learned scores as ranks, priorities, predictions, or heuristic guidance. |
| Evidence language | Claims should match the strength and maturity of the evidence. | Computational claims in the exemplars follow defined instances, baselines, and comparison protocols. | Distinguish frozen experiment evidence, exact-equivalence gates, diagnostics, and planned learning experiments. Use `TBD` for missing learning results and never convert a diagnostic replay into a performance conclusion. |
| Experimental narrative | Exact algorithms require both correctness and performance evaluation. | The exemplars report benchmark provenance, comparisons, algorithmic components, and operational or sensitivity analyses. | Separate proof-equivalence tests from runtime/closure tests. Require no-guidance, pricing-only, and pricing-plus-branching controls; report overhead and fallback rates, held-out maps/scales, and paired statistics before claiming benefit. Do not define a learned-cut ablation. |
| Operational interpretation | Results should return to transportation-system consequences. | TRC papers translate model or algorithm behavior into fleet, charging, synchronization, reliability, or resource-use implications. | Interpret validated results through feasible mission coverage, resource/risk allocation, fleet utilization, and planning reliability. Do not infer scientific water-ice yield from routing metrics alone. |
| Limitations and transfer | Transferability and open-science considerations should be visible. | The target venue emphasizes open data and benchmarking; exemplars expose dataset and evaluation roles. | State the three-path-per-directed-edge solution space, fixed benchmark scales, map provenance, host-memory boundary, and learning generalization limits. Identify what would be needed to transfer beyond the current lunar maps. |
| Voice and diction | Formal, compact, mechanism-centered English is appropriate. | Exemplars favor precise verbs and defined technical terms. | Avoid first person. Use “this paper,” “the proposed method,” or “the study”; use bounded verbs such as “formulates,” “implements,” “evaluates,” and “proves.” |
| Citation behavior | Journal positioning requires current direct competitors, foundations, application evidence, and limitations. | Exemplars connect each literature block to a concrete model or method choice. | Use the citation bank selectively. Treat You et al. (2026) and Abouelrous et al. (2025) as mandatory novelty checks for learned branching/pricing; cite lunar planning separately from exact-routing method precedents. |
