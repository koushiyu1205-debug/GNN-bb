## Verified support

- The article states that time-dependent, particularly seasonal, shadows and
  sunlit areas are important for lunar polar landing-site selection, solar
  power use, and traverse design.
- It calculates high-resolution illumination rates for a
  \(15\,\mathrm{km}\times15\,\mathrm{km}\) region near Shackleton crater
  during the lunar southern summer.
- It therefore demonstrates an operational use of a season-conditioned
  illumination analysis rather than treating a long-term average as adequate
  for every mission period.
- The study considers one southern-summer period; it does not compare four
  equinox-to-solstice phases and does not identify a fastest routing phase.

## Allowed manuscript inference

The paper supports treating the mission season as a controlled environmental
factor when evaluating lunar south-pole routes and operations. Combined with
C063's full-year equinox/solstice analysis, it supports independently
constructing fixed instances for several seasonal phases and comparing them
under common operational inputs.

The four equal equinox-to-solstice groups, three anchors per group, paired task
families, objective metrics, and exact-solver acceptance rules remain design
choices of the present experiment. They must not be attributed to Wei et al.

## Prohibited use

- Do not state that Wei et al. prescribe a four-phase routing experiment.
- Do not state that they compare spring, summer, autumn, and winter routing
  completion times.
- Do not infer that southern summer is the best or fastest phase.
- Do not transfer their 15 km by 15 km study extent to the present 50 km by
  50 km benchmark.
- Do not use this source to support BPC exactness, solver performance, or the
  adequacy of the present environmental aggregation rule.
